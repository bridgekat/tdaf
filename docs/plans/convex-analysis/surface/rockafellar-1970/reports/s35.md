## 2. `∂₁K`, `∂₂K`, `∂K`, `K′` — the names, and where the sign flip sits

**This is the section §37 needs. Read it whole.**

### `K′` is not renamed

`K′(u, v; u′, v′)` is `dirDerivReal K (u, v) (u′, v′)`, the backbone's
(`Tdaf/Analysis/Convex/Saddle/Differential.lean`). No surface `abbrev` was introduced for it: the
name differs but nothing has to be supplied — no pairing, no set — and §33's report warns that a
renaming `abbrev` is opaque to `rw`. Adding one here would have bought nothing and cost every later
`rw [dirDerivReal_prod_fst]`.

Note that `dirDerivReal` is a genuine **limit**, not `Subgradient/Defs.lean`'s `dirDeriv`, which is
an infimum of difference quotients. The two agree along a line for a convex function and differ in
a joint direction for a saddle-function — which is exactly what Theorem 35.6 is about — so any §37
statement about `K′` must use `dirDerivReal`.

### `∂₁K`, `∂₂K`, `∂K`: two layers, both exported

The book defines `∂K` at 15119–15155 for an **arbitrary** concave-convex `K` on `ℝᵐ × ℝⁿ` — hence
`EReal`-valued — testing against *all* of `ℝᵐ` and `ℝⁿ`. That is the definition §37 inherits, and
it is what the three surface `abbrev`s carry:

| book | surface `abbrev` | backbone |
|---|---|---|
| `∂₁K (u, v)` | `Rockafellar.subgrad₁ K p` | `concaveSubgradient (pairing m) (fun u => K (u, p.2)) p.1` |
| `∂₂K (u, v)` | `Rockafellar.subgrad₂ K p` | `subgradient (pairing n) (fun v => K (p.1, v)) p.2` |
| `∂K (u, v)` | `Rockafellar.subgrad K p` | `saddleSubgradient (pairing m) (pairing n) K p` |

all with `{m n : ℕ}`, `K : Rn m × Rn n → EReal`, `p : Rn m × Rn n`, and all **reducible `abbrev`s**
— so they *are* the backbone objects (`Tdaf/Analysis/Convex/Saddle/Subgradient.lean`), introduced
only to supply the Euclidean pairing. `subgrad_eq_prod : subgrad K p = subgrad₁ K p ×ˢ subgrad₂ K p`
is `rfl`. The §33 reducible-`abbrev` caveat applies verbatim: `exact` sees through them, `rw` with a
lemma stated in `concaveSubgradient` / `saddleSubgradient` will not fire.

Theorems 35.7–35.10 are about a `K` that is **finite** on an open rectangle, and there they use the
backbone's rectangle-relative, real-valued family from `Saddle/Differential.lean` —
`subgradientFst C K p`, `subgradientSnd D K p`, `subgradientSaddle C D K p` — used verbatim, with
no surface wrapper, because no pairing has to be supplied (they are stated against `⟪·,·⟫` on
`Rn m`). The bridge between the two layers is proved here at `C = D = univ`:

* `subgradFst_univ_eq : subgradientFst univ K p = subgrad₁ (fun z => ((K z : ℝ) : EReal)) p`
* `subgradSnd_univ_eq`, `subgradSaddle_univ_eq` — likewise.

For `C ≠ univ` the two differ: the rectangle-relative one tests only against `C` and `D`, and the
two agree once `K` is extended off `C × D` by the simple extension of `Saddle/Kernel.lean`
(`-∞`/`+∞` there, making the extra inequalities vacuous). **That extension bridge is not proved
here** — see the backbone gap in §3.

### Where the sign flip sits

**In `∂₁K`, and only there.** `subgrad₂` is `subgradient`, the ordinary convex subdifferential, with
the inequality

`K (u, v) + ⟨v*, v′ − v⟩ ≤ K (u, v′)`.

`subgrad₁` is `concaveSubgradient`, a *super*differential, with the inequality **reversed**:

`K (u′, v) ≤ K (u, v) + ⟨u*, u′ − u⟩`.

The one lemma §37 should cite, exported here under a book-facing name:

```lean
theorem mem_subgrad₁_iff_neg_mem_subgradient_neg {y : Rn m} :
    y ∈ subgrad₁ K p ↔ -y ∈ subgradient (pairing m) (fun u => -K (u, p.2)) p.1
```

(it is `mem_concaveSubgradient_iff_neg_mem_subgradient_neg` at the Euclidean pairing; the companion
`neg_mem_concaveSubgradient_iff` puts the negation on the other side). **That `u* ↦ −u*` is the
whole of the sign.** It is why `∂K` is not the subdifferential of `K` read as a function on
`ℝᵐ⁺ⁿ`, why Corollary 37.5.2 must insert `u* ↦ −u*` to obtain monotonicity, and why Corollary
37.5.1's homeomorphism is the asymmetric `(u − u*, v + v*)`. Nothing in §35 flips it a second time:
`subgradientFst`'s backbone bridge `subgradientFst_eq_neg_subgradient` carries the same single
negation, and `subgrad = subgrad₁ ×ˢ subgrad₂` is a plain product with no reordering.

On the real-valued side the gradient is a **pair**, not a vector: `HasSaddleGradientAt K q p` is
`HasFDerivAt K (prodInnerL q) p` with `q : Rn m × Rn n`, because Mathlib gives a product of
inner-product spaces the *supremum* norm and `Rn m × Rn n` is therefore not an inner-product space.
The same choice makes every `εB` in Theorems 35.7, 35.9 and 35.10 the supremum ball; the book's
Euclidean ball differs by a factor bounded by `√2` and every statement is over all `ε > 0`.

## 3. Backbone gaps and friction

  Wanted, in `Tdaf/Analysis/Convex/Saddle/Differential.lean` beside `subgradientSnd_eq_subgradient`:
  ```lean

      (Bu : U →ₗ[ℝ] V →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ) (Bx : X →ₗ[ℝ] Y →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ) (hp : p ∈ C ×ˢ D) :
  ```

## 8. Gotchas worth adding to `gotchas.md`

**SET (or LIB) — `(volume : Measure (E × F)).IsAddHaarMeasure` is not found by instance search.**
Mathlib has `IsAddHaarMeasure (volume : Measure E)` for a finite-dimensional inner-product space
and `MeasureTheory.Measure.prod.instIsAddHaarMeasure` for a product of Haar measures, but the
composition through `Prod.instMeasureSpace` is not automatic. Supply it:
`have : (volume : Measure (E × F)).IsAddHaarMeasure := Measure.prod.instIsAddHaarMeasure volume volume`.
Use `have`, not `haveI` — the `linter.style.haveILetI` linter rejects `haveI` on a proposition, and
a local hypothesis of class type is registered as a local instance either way. Cost: ~10 minutes.

**LINT — an unused hypothesis kept for faithfulness must be bound as `_hC`, not `hC`.** Surface
statements routinely carry a hypothesis the backbone does not need (Theorem 35.6 keeps `Convex ℝ C`
because the book states it, and uses only `IsOpen C`). Naming it `hC` trips the unused-variable
linter and fails the zero-warning bar; the leading underscore is the whole fix, and the docstring
should say why the hypothesis is there. Established practice: `Rockafellar.corollary_10_5_2`'s
`_hg`.
