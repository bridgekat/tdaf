`bifunBracket F` and `adjointBracket F` are the pair `(K̲, K̄)`; both are **uncurried**, functions
of `(u, x*)`, because that is the form every closure operation and every closedness predicate is
stated against. Their defining formulas are `bifunBracket_apply`, `concaveBifunBracket_apply`,
`bifunOfSaddleFn_apply`, all `rfl`.

### The orientation, restated for the record

`cl₁` = `partialCl₁` closes the **concave** argument (the **first**) **concavely**.
`cl₂` = `partialCl₂` closes the **convex** argument (the **second**) **convexly**.
`LowerClosedFn K` is `partialCl₂ (partialCl₁ K) = K` — `cl₁` **innermost**.
`UpperClosedFn K` is `partialCl₁ (partialCl₂ K) = K` — `cl₁` **outermost**.
The module docstring states this in a table before any Lean, per the brief.

**One thing that surprised me and is worth flagging to §§34–37**: `saddleSwap` does **not** convert
a convex-concave function into a concave-convex one — it maps concave-convex to concave-convex and
convex-concave to convex-concave (`concaveConvexFn_saddleSwap`). The translation for Rockafellar's
convex-concave convention (14411) is **plain negation**, not the swap: for convex-concave `K`,
book-`cl₁ K = -(cl₁ (-K))` and book-`cl₂ K = -(cl₂ (-K))`, hence the book's *lower closed* is
`UpperClosedFn (-K)` and the book's *upper closed* is `LowerClosedFn (-K)`. That is
`convexConcave_lowerClosed_iff` / `convexConcave_upperClosed_iff`. Anyone reaching for `saddleSwap`
here will silently get the wrong statement.

### Reducible-`abbrev` caveat for the next agents

`cl₁`, `cl₂`, `bifunBracket`, `adjointBracket`, `bifunOfSaddleFn`, `dualProgram` all elaborate to
constants of their own, so `exact` and unification see through them but **`rw` with a backbone
lemma stated in `partialCl₁` / `bracket` / `adjointBifun` will not fire**. The two idioms that
work: restate through a `have` with the surface type (`have e : partialCl₁ K = L := h`), or
`show`/`change` to the backbone form. Both are used in this file
(`corollary_33_3_1_lowerClosed`, `corollary_33_3_3_dom`).

* **Theorem 33.3's proof asserts without argument** that "for image-closed convex bifunctions `F`,

  in `Saddle/Correspondence.lean`. Recorded in `theorem_33_3`.

* **Corollary 33.3.3's hypothesis is weakened.** The book asks for `K` "finite continuous" on
  `C × D`, i.e. jointly continuous. The argument uses only separate continuity of each slice on its
  own set, and that is what the statements ask for (`hcontD`, `hcontC`). The Lean hypothesis is
  therefore strictly weaker than the book's. Recorded in the `Cor3333` section docstring.

* **Theorem 33.1's convex-closedness clause needs no hypothesis on `F` at all.** The book states the
  whole theorem for a convex bifunction; `theorem_33_1_convexClosed` and `theorem_33_1_imageClosed`
  take none, because a conjugate is closed whatever it is a conjugate of. Recorded in each.

## 5. Gotchas worth adding to `gotchas.md`

**EL — reducible `abbrev`s block `rw`.** A surface `abbrev` that renames a backbone operator
(`Rockafellar.cl₁` for `partialCl₁`, `Rockafellar.dualProgram` for `adjointBifun …`) is transparent
to `exact` and to unification but **opaque to `rw`**: the goal's head constant is the new name, so
`rw [partialCl₁_slice]` reports "did not find an occurrence". Fix by restating the hypothesis with
a typed `have` (`have e : partialCl₁ K = L := h`) or by `show`ing the backbone form — never by
adding an unfolding `simp` lemma, which then fires everywhere. Cost: ~15 minutes.

**BLD — a `<<'EOF'` heredoc carrying a large Lean file through the Bash tool failed** with
`unexpected EOF while looking for matching ''` even though the delimiter was quoted. Writing the
chunk to the scratchpad with the `Write` tool and `cat`-ing it onto the target worked first time
and is the reliable pattern for appending to a Lean file.
