**`Ω` is the only new surface definition.** Signature:

```
Rockafellar.Ω {m n : ℕ} (F : Bifun (Rn m) (Rn n)) : Set (Rn m × Rn n → EReal) :=
  {K | ConcaveConvexFn K} ∩ saddleClass (bifunBracket F) (adjointBracket F)
```

with `mem_Ω : K ∈ Ω F ↔ ConcaveConvexFn K ∧ bifunBracket F ≤ K ∧ K ≤ adjointBracket F` (`Iff.rfl`).

**Why the intersection.** Rockafellar's `Ω (F)` (14659) is "the collection of all *concave-convex*
functions `K` such that `K̲ ≤ K ≤ K̄`". The backbone's `bifunSaddleClass` is the order interval
**without** the concave-convexity. Dropping the condition would have been a mistranslation —
Theorem 34.2's "`Ω (F)` is an equivalence class" is false for the bare interval, because a
non-concave-convex function between the brackets is not in any equivalence class of
concave-convex functions. So `Ω` carries it, and `mem_bifunSaddleClass_of_mem_Ω` (which is
`hK.2`) is what feeds every §37 backbone lemma stated against `bifunSaddleClass`:
`dom₁_eq_domBifun_of_mem_bifunSaddleClass`, `partialCl₁_lowerConjSaddle`,
`upperConjSaddle_eq_concaveBracket_adjointBifun`, and the rest of `Saddle/Conjugate.lean`.

**§37 should quantify over `F` (or over `Ω F`), never over a bare saddle-function.** That is the
whole design consequence of Theorem 34.2, and it is why `Ω` opens this module before Theorem 34.1.
The pointwise recovery formulas are there for when a class member has to be turned back into the
bifunction: `theorem_34_2_bifunOfSaddleFn : bifunOfSaddleFn K = F`, with
`theorem_34_2_bifun_apply` and `theorem_34_2_adjoint` as the two displayed formulas.

Also worth knowing downstream: `theorem_34_2_dom₁` and `theorem_34_2_dom₂` are the identification
`C = dom F`, `D = dom F*` that §37 uses silently everywhere; both need a nonemptiness hypothesis
the book suppresses (see §4 below).

## 4. Book divergences recorded in docstrings

* **Theorem 34.2's `dom K = dom F × dom F*` is a *product* identity and only that.** The book's
  proof (14735) argues the factors separately — "`u ∉ dom₁ K` if and only if `Fu` is the constant
  `+∞`, i.e. `u ∉ dom F`" — and that factorwise claim is **false when `F` is improper**. If the
  graph function of `F` is identically `+∞` then `K̲ = K̄ ≡ −∞`, so `dom₁ K = ∅ = dom F` but
  `dom₂ K = ℝⁿ` while `dom F* = ∅`; the product identity survives because both sides are empty,
  the factor identity does not. `theorem_34_2_dom₁` and `theorem_34_2_dom₂` therefore carry the
  nonemptiness hypotheses the book suppresses, and `theorem_34_2_dom` is derived from them under
  `ProperSaddleFn K`. Recorded in the module docstring and in `theorem_34_2_dom₁`. This is not in
  `part7.md`'s hazard list and I did not expect it.
* **Corollaries 34.2.1 and 34.2.3 are printed with no proof at all** (14761, 14819). Both derived
  from Theorem 34.2; recorded in their section docstrings.
* **Corollary 34.2.1's `dom L = dom K` clause needs no closedness**, only properness of both
  functions, although the book states the whole corollary under "let `K` be a closed
  saddle-function". `corollary_34_2_1_dom_of_not_closed` records that explicitly. (The *second*
  clause does need closedness of both.)
* **Theorem 34.1 needs no hypothesis whatsoever.** The book proves it through Theorems 33.1, 33.2,
  33.3 and 30.1 for a saddle-function; `lowerCl_idem` / `upperCl_idem` reprove it from
  monotonicity and idempotence alone, so `theorem_34_1_lower` and `theorem_34_1_upper` quantify
  over *every* `K : Rn m × Rn n → EReal`, strictly stronger than the book. Recorded in each.
* **Corollary 34.2.4's continuity hypothesis is weakened** from joint to separate continuity of
  each slice on its own set, the same weakening §33 recorded for Corollary 33.3.3; and the proof
  route differs from the book's, which goes through Corollary 33.3.3 while the backbone closes it
  directly from the closedness of the slices of the two simple extensions. Recorded in the module
  docstring and the `Cor3424` section docstring.
* **Theorem 34.2's proof begins 34 lines after its statement** (14657 vs 14691); the intervening
  text is the `Ω (F*) = Ω (F)` discussion and is not part of the proof. Noted in the module
  docstring so the next reader does not repeat the mistake.
* **Theorem 34.2's fourth formula is not the third one "by symmetry"**, as the book's silence

## 7. Gotchas for `gotchas.md`

**EL — a surface `abbrev` blocks `rw` in *both* directions.** §33 recorded the case where the goal
carries the surface name and the lemma the backbone name. The mirror case bites too: when the goal
is `dualProgram F x v` and the lemma is about `adjointBifun Bu Bx F y v`, `rw` reports "motive is
not type correct"/"did not find an occurrence" even though the two are the same constant applied
to the same arguments. The reliable fix is to state the whole equation as a `have` using the
**backbone** head constant and close the goal with `exact`, never `simp [dualProgram]` (which
unfolds everywhere and then leaves `bifunBracket` and `adjointBracket` in the goal in expanded
form). Cost: ~10 minutes.
