## 2. The orientation pair

**The Part plan and the task brief are wrong on one point of fact: the backbone does *not* carry
the orientation as a field.** `Bifunction/Process.lean`'s design note is explicit — "**Orientation
is a second adjoint, not a flag.** … Rather than bundle an orientation into the structure — which
would double every statement — the two adjoints are two definitions, `adjointProcess` and
`coadjointProcess`". `ConvexProcess` is a one-field structure wrapping a `PointedCone`, and
`grep -rn "orient" Tdaf/Analysis/Convex/` finds the word only in docstrings.

That choice is right for the backbone and insufficient for the surface, exactly as the plan
predicted for a different reason. So the pair was built here:

* `Rockafellar.Orientation` — `inductive` with `sup` / `inf`, `deriving DecidableEq`; plus
  `Orientation.flip` and `flip_sup` / `flip_inf` / `flip_flip`.
* `Rockafellar.OrientedProcess m n` — `@[ext] structure` with fields `process` and `orientation`.
  This is Rockafellar's "pair consisting of a convex set and one of the words" (16945), verbatim.
* `Rockafellar.Orientation.bracketSet` — `⟨C, x*⟩` as a `match` on the word, with
  `bracketSet_sup_eq_supportFn` and `bracketSet_inf_eq_neg_supportFn` as the two bridges the book
  gives at 16955 and 16957.
* `Rockafellar.Orientation.adjointProcess` — the dispatch, with `adjointProcess_sup` and
  `adjointProcess_inf` both `rfl`.
* `OrientedProcess.adjoint`, `.inv`, `Add`, `SMul`, `.comp` — the operations, each with `rfl`
  projection lemmas. `adjoint` flips the orientation; `inv` flips it; `Add`, `SMul` and `comp`
  take the orientation of their left argument, matching 16963.
* `OrientedProcess.bracket` — `⟨Au, x*⟩`, with `bracket_sup` (bridge to
  `bracket (pairing n) A.indicatorBifun`, via `ConvexProcess.bracket_indicatorBifun_apply`) and
  `bracket_inf` (`rfl` onto `ConvexProcess.coBracket`), plus the `_fn`, `_arg` and `_prod`
  eta-variants that the `rw`-syntactic-matching rule (gotcha EL3) forces.

**It carried cleanly, and it paid for itself three times.**

* `theorem_39_2_orientation` is `rfl` — "`A*` has the opposite orientation" is not a remark, it is
  the definition of `adjoint`, and it is *statable*.
* `theorem_39_5` and `theorem_39_8` take `hor : A₁.orientation = A₂.orientation` and
  `hor : B.orientation = A.orientation` and discharge them by `subst`; every proof is then two
  `cases` branches, one per backbone lemma. Without the pair, `hor` cannot be written.
* `theorem_39_3_posHomogeneous` and `theorem_39_3_posHomogeneous_arg` come out **orientation-free**
  — positive homogeneity holds in both orientations — while `theorem_39_3_convexFn` /
  `_concaveFn` and `_concaveFn_arg` / `_convexFn_arg` come out as *four* declarations that the
  reader can see are the book's "except that then convexity and concavity are reversed". A flag
  would have hidden which clauses are orientation-sensitive; two unrelated definitions would have
  hidden that they are the same clause.

The one cost: `A.adjoint.bracket y u` is `⟨u, A* x*⟩` with the arguments in the process's own
order, so the two private bridges `bracket_adjoint_sup` and `bracket_adjoint_inf` each pay one
`pairing_comm` under a binder. Four lines each. That is the entire overhead of the apparatus.

## 3. Backbone gaps and friction

### 3.1 The serious one: Theorems 39.5's and 39.8's hypotheses exclude §39's own example

`ConvexProcess.adjointProcess_add` (`Bifunction/Process.lean:1146`) asks for

```lean
hex : ∀ y : Y, IsExactSum Bu (fun u => -(supportFn Bx (A₁.eval u) y))
                             (fun u => -(supportFn Bx (A₂.eval u) y))
```

`IsExactSum` (`Duality/Exact.lean:185`) has `proper_left`/`proper_right`, and `Proper`
(`Epigraph.lean:127`) requires `f x ≠ ⊥` **everywhere**. So the hypothesis asserts, for every `x*`
and every `u`, that `⟨Aᵢ u, x*⟩ ≠ +∞` — i.e. `dom Aᵢ* = ℝⁿ`.

Rockafellar's hypothesis is `ri (dom A₁) ∩ ri (dom A₂) ≠ ∅`. The backbone's design note says the
reduction "is now available and simply has not been done", via `IsExactSum.of_relint`. **It is not
available.** `IsExactSum.of_relint` (`Duality/Relint.lean:436`) takes `Proper f` and `Proper g` as
arguments, and here they are false.

Concretely, for §39's own leading example (16847), `Au = {x | x ≤ Bu}` for `u ≥ 0` and `∅`
otherwise: `A0 = {x | x ≤ 0}`, so `⟨A0, x*⟩ = +∞` for every `x*` with a negative coordinate, and
`hex` fails at that `x*`. Since Theorem 39.1 says a convex process with full domain and bounded
`A0` *is* a linear transformation, the hypothesis is close to "`Aᵢ` is a linear transformation".
`ConvexProcess.adjointProcess_comp` (:1288), `coadjointProcess_add` (:1795),
`coadjointProcess_comp` (:1811), and the four closed halves inherit the same defect, as does
`Bifunction/Algebra.lean`'s `adjointBifun_infConvBifun_eq_supConvBifun` (:347), whose docstring
claims "the hypothesis is Rockafellar's exactly" — true for the *pointwise* form at one `y`
(where properness is his branch condition `y ∈ dom F₁* ∩ dom F₂*`), false once it is quantified
over all `y`.

**What is wanted.** Either

* `ConvexProcess.adjointProcess_add_of_relint` in `Bifunction/Process.lean`, with hypothesis
  `∃ u ∈ ri A₁.dom, u ∈ ri A₂.dom` (Rockafellar's), proved by splitting on
  `x* ∈ dom A₁* ∩ dom A₂*` — the branch where the exactness hypothesis is vacuous being handled
  by both sides collapsing — and mirrors `coadjointProcess_add_of_relint`,
  `adjointProcess_comp_of_relint`, `coadjointProcess_comp_of_relint`; or
* a weakening of `IsExactSum` that tolerates `⊥`-valued summands, which is a `Duality/Exact.lean`
  change with library-wide consequences and is almost certainly the wrong fix.

The surface transcribes the backbone hypothesis verbatim (BRIEF's "write the surface statement
anyway, close it however you can, and record the friction") and says so in `theorem_39_5`'s and
`theorem_39_8`'s docstrings and in the module's `## Where the book's hypotheses had to change`.
No weaker statement was invented and numbered as the book's.

### 3.2 `ConvexProcess.concaveAdjointBifun_neg_indicatorBifun` — missing

Theorem 39.2's last assertion has no infimum-oriented half in the backbone.
`adjointBifun_indicatorBifun` (`Process.lean:784`) covers the supremum orientation. The mirror is

```lean
theorem ConvexProcess.concaveAdjointBifun_neg_indicatorBifun
    (Bu : U →ₗ[ℝ] V →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ) (Bx : X →ₗ[ℝ] Y →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ) (A : ConvexProcess U X) (y : Y) (v : V) :
    concaveAdjointBifun Bx Bu (fun u x => -(A.indicatorBifun u x)) y v
      = (coadjointProcess Bu Bx A).indicatorBifun y v
```

(`concaveAdjointBifun` is in `Optimization/Adjoint.lean:465`; the pairing arguments come out
swapped because a concave bifunction from `U` to `X` has its adjoint read backwards.) It is a
~20-line direct computation on the cone — the supremum over `graph A` of `⟨x, w⟩ − ⟨u, z⟩` is `0`
on the coadjoint graph and `+∞` off it — so it is backbone work, not surface work. Home:
`Tdaf/Analysis/Convex/Bifunction/Process.lean`. Recorded in the module's `## What is not here`.

1. **Theorem 39.1: `A0 = {0}` in place of "`A0` bounded".** The book's hypothesis is boundedness;
   the first line of his proof converts it to `A0 = {0}` and nothing afterwards uses boundedness.
   `theorem_39_1` takes `hzero : A.eval 0 = {0}`, and `theorem_39_1_isBounded` recovers the book's
   literal form through `ConvexProcess.eval_zero_eq_zero_of_isBounded`, so the generalisation costs
   nothing. Docstring follows the Cor 32.3.3 precedent in `Part6/Section32.lean`. (Note the
   backbone lemma itself, `exists_linearMap_of_isBounded`, still carries `Bornology.IsBounded` and
   a `NormedAddCommGroup X`; restating it with `A.eval 0 = {0}` over a bare `Module ℝ X` would drop
   the norm from `Bifunction/Process.lean:1445` entirely. Worth doing.)
2. **Theorems 39.5, 39.7, 39.8: `IsExactSum` in place of the relative-interior condition.**
   §3.1 above. Recorded in the module docstring and in each of the three docstrings.
3. **Theorem 39.3's last assertion, `u` half: no closedness.** The book prefixes both halves with
   "if `A` is closed"; the `u` half is Corollary 33.2.1 and needs none.
   `theorem_39_3_relint_dom` is stated without it — a strengthening, recorded in its docstring.
   `theorem_39_3_relint_dom_adjoint` (the `x*` half) does carry it.
4. **Theorem 39.2's `A** = cl A` is stated at the level of graphs**, `A.adjoint.adjoint.process.graph = closure A.process.graph`, rather than as `A** = cl A` with a
   `cl` operation on processes. The backbone has no `ConvexProcess.cl`; the graph equation is the
   same statement and is what the bipolar theorem produces. `theorem_39_2_eq_self_iff` gives the
   process-level form for closed `A`.
5. **Corollary 39.7.1 is proved from Theorem 9.1, not Theorem 39.7.** Recorded in the docstring;
   the observation is the backbone's, not mine (`Process.lean` design note).

## 5. Surprises

* **The backbone was more complete than the plan implied.** Both orientations of Theorems 39.2,
  39.3 (first three assertions), 39.5, 39.6 and 39.8 are present, and the §2.2 de-leak really is
  closed — `reflect` occurs in no hypothesis of `Bifunction/Process.lean`, so
  `coadjointProcess_add` and `coadjointProcess_comp` were usable verbatim in `theorem_39_5` and
  `theorem_39_8` with no transport. Every one of the nine results closed in one to five lines.
* **`Rockafellar.Orientation` does not clash with Mathlib's `Orientation`** even though
  `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Orientation` is in the import closure: the enclosing `namespace
  Rockafellar` wins. No rename was needed.
* **The plan's claim that the backbone "carries [orientation] as a field" is false**, and so is
  the same sentence in the task brief and in `inventory.md`'s §39 entry. Worth correcting in
  `part8.md` — the correct statement is that the backbone deliberately does *not*, and that the
  surface must therefore build the pair itself. That is the finding, not a problem.
* **`Equiv.completeLattice` exists in Mathlib and just works.** The complete lattice of 16943 cost
  a six-line `Equiv` and one application; no order-theoretic content was rebuilt.

## 6. For `gotchas.md`

Nothing cost twenty minutes. Two small entries worth having:

* **EL (rewriting).** `simpa using h` is the wrong tool for turning `Bx.flip`/`Bu.flip` back into
  `pairing n`/`pairing m` when the goal also contains `closure ↑A.graph` — `simp` will chase the
  `SetLike` coercion. `simp only [flip_pairing] at h; exact h` is the safe form, and it is what
  `graph_biadjoint_sup`/`graph_biadjoint_inf` and the five `simpa only [flip_pairing] using hex`
  sites in this section use.
* **LINT (deprecations).** `Set.mem_setOf_eq` is deprecated in this Mathlib in favour of
  `Set.mem_ofPred_eq`. It is the only deprecation this section tripped, and it fires inside a
  `simp only` list where the name is easy to copy from an older surface module.
